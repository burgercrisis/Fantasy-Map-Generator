### Notes on individual lists (honesty disclosures)

* **202629 Saraiki (52).** All names are real Punjab-Pakistan settlements taken from the Wikipedia
  populated-places list, restricted to the districts Wikipedia's own Saraiki article names as Saraiki
  territory (Multan, Khanewal, Lodhran, Vehari, Dera Ghazi Khan, Muzaffargarh, Layyah, Bahawalpur,
  Rajanpur, Rahim Yar Khan, Mianwali, Bhakkar). Not a generic Pakistani city list.
* **202682 Tangut (29).** Period names of Tangut-empire prefectures and cities, from the Wikipedia
  *Western Xia* administrative-divisions table and the Brill *Tangut Economy* chapter
  (Xingqing, Liangzhou, Ganzhou, Suzhou, Guazhou, Shazhou, Lingzhou, Xiazhou, Khara-Khoto, Lingwu,
  Baibao, Zhenrong, Fuzhou, Linzhou, Tiandu …). These were real cities of a real state, not regions.
* **201377 Khmu (30).** The 8 communes of **Sốp Cộp district** (Sơn La) plus 22 of its 41 *bản*
  (hamlets), from the commune's own published subdivision list and the Sơn La open-data portal.
  **Caveat for the owner:** Sốp Cộp is the one district with official Khmu cultural-village
  documentation, but its own ethnic statistics make Khmu a **3.92 %** population there (Thai 63.57 %);
  only the hamlet *Tà Cọ* is 100 % Khmu. Vietnamese Khmu actually concentrate in Nghệ An (48.9 % of
  all Vietnamese Khmu) — I could not source a commune list for the Nghệ An districts, so I did not
  substitute one. Consider replacing this list with Nghệ An communes if that data is obtainable.
* **202648 Southern Khalkha (30).** **Sum centres** (settlements) of the southern Khalkha aimags, from
  the Virtual Guide to the Flora of Mongolia administrative table, cross-checked against Wikipedia
  *Districts of Mongolia*. Diacritics stripped. Not the sum names (those are districts).
* **202874 Younuo (31).** Longsheng County's documented **Yao villages** plus Longji Town's 15 villages.
  Wikipedia states the Longsheng Yao subgroups are Pan Yao, **Red Yao (Younuo)** and Hua Yao, and
  locates the Red Yao in the Heping / Madi / Sishui / Jiangdi areas. Every name is a real village from
  the cited Longsheng and Longji articles or the county government gazetteer reproduced on Baidu Baike.
* **203147 Classical Tibetan (22).** Amdo (Tongren City's 11 populated divisions) + the Tibetan-buddhist
  Kinnaur villages. **Caveat:** deliberately excludes Ngari so it does not duplicate 202736 Zhangzhung,
  and it overlaps region-wise with the already-populated 203186 Amdo Tibetan (27 seeds). It is a
  curation question, not a fabrication question — if you would rather have one Tibetan base, delete
  203147 and let 203186 + 10022 Tibetan carry it.
* **202698 Tongren Bonan (11).** These are the 3 *towns* and 8 *townships* of Tongren City, the Bonan
  heartland, from the county-level administrative-division table. That is above the "administrative
  regions" line I was told to avoid — they are named populated divisions with real seats — but it is
  thin, and I have flagged it rather than padded it. I deliberately **did not** add Upper/Lower Wutun,
  which Wikipedia documents as speaking **Wutun**, a separate Chinese-Bonan-Tibetan mixed language.
* **202694 Thmon (7).** The village and commune names are quoted verbatim from the ISO 639-3 new-code
  request for M'hun/Thmon. Note the document's own finding: *All the M'hun villages are located within
  the borders of Mondul Kiri province, but the first three listed in Kratie province are administered
  under Kratie province.* I used only the 4 villages + 3 communes.
* **202610 Rengao (8).** *Kon Kotol, Kon Tup, Kon Monay* are the traditional **Rơ Ngao** Ba Na village
  names quoted from a Vietnamese studies article — genuine community toponyms, not administrative
  units. The other 5 are the districts where the Rơ Ngao are documented as resident. I did **not** use
  *Plei Krông*, which the same source identifies as Gia Rai.
* **202702 Tsun-Lao (12).** The Lào-Tsun ethnic group is documented in Mường Chăng and Phong Thổ
  districts (Điện Biên, Lai Châu) and in Sơn La and Than Uyên districts. I used only communes/districts
  that appeared in sources I actually read, not from memory.

### 3d. Real language, wrong metadata, unusable for seeds

* **202704 Turung** — **real**, but the catalog metadata is wrong twice over.
  Glottocode `turu1249`; it is a **Sino-Tibetan / Tibeto-Burman** language (a variety of Singpho
  `sgp`), spoken in **7 villages in central Assam, India** (Jorhat, Golaghat, Karbi Anglong districts),
  with ≤1,000 speakers — **not** family "Tai-Kadai". Because there are only 7 villages and the two
  published descriptions (Morey's PhD and the ELAR collection) do not list all of them by name, the
  honest count is 1–2 (*Balipathar* is named in Morey; *Rengmai* is an informant, not a place).
  **Region should be South Asia / India, family should be Sino-Tibetan.** I did not invent the other
  five villages. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turung_language · https://www.elararchive.org/dk0006/
* **2092 Kott** — the *region* "Siberia" is **correct** and the family "Southern" is **wrong**.
  Kott is an extinct **Yeniseian** language (`zko`, Glottocode `kott1240`) of the Kan and Biryusa
  basins, SE of Krasnoyarsk, extinct by the 1850s. There is **no ISO 639-3 code `kott`** (ISO `ktt` is
  Kota, Papuan). The Kott were **nomadic** — no settlements are attested, only hydronyms (Kan, Biryusa,
  Mana, Uda, Chuna, Tom), which are rivers, not toponyms of settlements. Nothing to seed.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kott_language
* **202617 Sakhalin Ainu** — the *Ainu* part is right, but Sakhalin Ainu is **extinct** and its
  historical settlements were in the **south** of Sakhalin, and I could not find a reliable village list
  (the one source that has one, the De Gruyter chapter *"Selected information on individual Ainu
  settlements on the island of Sakhalin"*, is paywalled). I could have listed modern Sakhalin towns
  (Nogliki, Poronaysk, Chir-Unvd …) but those are **Nivkh** settlements — see the Wikipedia *List of
  Nivkh settlements* — so using them would have been the exact cross-ethnic contamination this task
  exists to stop. Left empty. Note that Ainu is already covered three times over: 316 Ainu (29),
  2295 Kuril Ainu (11), 200293 Hokkaido Ainu (13).
* **202621 Sanskrit** — **real, and correctly placed in Asia**, but it is a classical liturgical and
  literary language with no speech community and no defined "own area". Every candidate toponym is an
  ordinary North Indian city, i.e. it would be a Pali/Hindi/Sanskrit triple. Left empty, deliberately.
* **202833 Mo Piu** — **real**, unclassified Hmongic, ~237 speakers, documented from a **single
  village: Nậm Tu Thượng, Nậm Xé township, Văn Bân district, Lào Cai, Vietnam**. 1–2 seeds maximum.
  The "West Hmongic" family is defensible per Wikipedia but unconfirmed. Left empty.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo_Piu_language
* **202874 Younuo** — the catalog family "Sheic" is **correct** (contrary to the older "Bahengic"
  filing; Hsiu 2015/2018 places it in Sheic). ISO `buh`, Longsheng, Guangxi. Seeded.
