## Research
- **Source**: Wikipedia — Koryo-mar (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koryo-mar), Koryo-saram (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koryo-saram)
- **Key finding**: Koryo-mar (고려말 / Корё мар) is a Korean dialect spoken by the Koryo-saram — ethnic Koreans in the former Soviet Union. It descends from the Hamgyŏng dialect of northeastern Korea. Koryo-saram were originally in the Russian Far East but were deported en masse to Central Asia (Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan) in 1937. The language was NEVER spoken in South Korea.
- **Regions**: Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Russia, Ukraine, Kyrgyzstan, Turkmenistan, Tajikistan — all former Soviet republics.
- **Notable communities**: Almaty (Kazakhstan), Tashkent (Uzbekistan), Vladivostok/Posyet/Nakhodka (Russian Far East), Volgograd (Russia), Bishkek (Kyrgyzstan), Ttaetgol/Hambak (Koryo-saram villages in South Korea — but these are RETURN migration settlements, not where Koryo-mar was historically spoken; however, Koryo-saram communities exist there now).
