# Tera (i=200190) — Verification Log

- **Slug:** `tera`
- **Language:** Tera / Nyimatli (ISO 639-3 `ttr`), Glottolog `tera1251`
- **Family:** Afro-Asiatic → Chadic → **Biu–Mandara (Central Chadic) A.1**, *not* West Chadic (the entry brief said "West Chadic" — corrected here per Wikipedia/Blench)
- **Country / Region:** Nigeria — north and eastern **Gombe State** (Yamaltu/Deba LGA, part of Kwami LGA) and adjacent **Borno State**
- **Status:** WAITING
- **Confidence:** MEDIUM
- **Result:** 17 verified names (from 63 original), min=4, max=9, d="", m=0

## Status

**WAITING.** The original entry was very heavily contaminated: it contained a full block of synthetic mixer tokens (`Terawa` … `Terabo`), a large block of Somali/Kenyan (East African) place names that have nothing to do with Nigeria, several Tangale/Waja/Pero/Bolewa towns from other Gombe LGAs, and language/dialect names used as settlements. After removing all of these, only **17** individually verified Tera-area settlements survive. Attempts to raise the count to 25 by adding villages from the Yamaltu/Deba locality lists ran into a hard evidence problem: Yamaltu/Deba LGA is documented as "**Tera and Fulani**", the LGA's "Jara Area" belongs to the separate **Jara** language community, and many remaining localities are transparently Hausa/Fulani quarter names (Sabon Gari, Tudun Wada, Nasarawo, Zamfarawa, Yelwa Kuri, Jarawa, Jigawa, Garin Nabawa, Tashar Kuka, Wudil). Because per Rule 5 no unverified name may be admitted, the entry is left at **WAITING with the verified subset**.

## Research Log

1. **Language identity and range.** English Wikipedia, *Tera language*: "Tera is a Chadic dialect cluster spoken in northeastern Nigeria in the north and eastern parts of **Gombe State** and **Borno State**." Classification: Afro-Asiatic → Chadic → **Biu–Mandara → Tera languages (A.1)**. ISO `ttr`, Glottolog `tera1251`, ~101,000 speakers (2000). Dialects: Nyimatli, Pidlimdi, Bura Kokura. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tera_language). Ethnologue's free page confirms `ttr` = Tera, Nigeria, Afro-Asiatic, vitality "Stable", NT 2016 (https://www.ethnologue.com/language/ttr/).
2. **Settlement anchor list.** English Wikipedia, *Tera people*: "they occupied settlements at the banks of River Gongola, such as **Gwani, Hinna, Liji, Kalshingi, Zambuk, Bage, Kurba, Doho, Deba** etc." (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tera_people). This is the single most useful primary anchor and supplied most of the retained core.
3. **LGA-level ethnic attribution.** English Wikipedia, *Yamaltu/Deba*: "Ethnicities: **Tera** and **Fulani**"; "It is inhabited by mainly Tera and Fulani people." Wards: Deba; Difa/Lubo/Kinafa; **Gwani/Shinga/Wade**; **Hinna**; Jagali North; Jagali South; Kanawa/Wajari; Kuri/Lano/Lambam; **Kwadon/Liji/Kurba**; Nono/Kunwal/W. Birdeka. Localities are grouped into three areas — **Deba**, **Jara Area**, **Yelmatul (Yamaltu) Area** — of which the *Jara Area* is named for the separate **Jara** language, so its localities were excluded. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamaltu/Deba)

   - Difa — 10.3554 N 11.4285 E
   - Wade — 10.4741 N 11.6181 E (alt. Wadi)
   - Gwani — 10.4204 N 11.4995 E
   - Shinga — 10.4633 N 11.5512 E

   - Nono — 10.1473 N 11.3861 E
   - Wajari — 10.2768 N 11.4044 E
   And in **Kwami LGA, Gombe**: Doho — 10.4299 N 11.2267 E.
5. **`Liji` and `Kurba`.** GeoNames returns "Liji Hill" (a 678 m hill in Yamaltu/Deba) rather than a settlement node, and its "Kurba" records are in Bauchi/Borno. Both are nonetheless retained because (a) Wikipedia's Tera-people article lists both as Tera settlements, (b) both appear in the Yamaltu/Deba **locality list**, and (c) both are components of the official ward name **"Kwadon / Liji / Kurba"** — three independent attestations that they are inhabited places in the Tera core. `Kurba` is additionally one of the three lects named in the Tera dialect entry.
6. **False-friend check on `Difa`.** `Difa` initially looked like the Niger town **Diffa**. It is not: GeoNames places Difa as a populated place at 10.3554 N 11.4285 E in Yamaltu/Deba, and it is the head of the Yamaltu/Deba ward "Difa / Lubo / Kinafa". Retained.
7. **Orthography normalisation.** The source list used Tera-orthography implosives (`Waɗe`, `Ɓalɓiya`). Tera's alphabet does use ɓ/ɗ/ɠ (Tench 2007), so these are not nonsense, but the *gazetteer* forms are `Wade` and `Balbaya/Balbiya`. `Wade` was normalised and retained (it is in the Tera area); `Ɓalɓiya` was removed because the place itself is in Bura/Babur territory in Borno.
8. **`Ghәna`.** The character between `h` and `n` is **U+04D9 CYRILLIC SMALL LETTER SCHWA**, not the Latin `ə` used in Nigerian orthographies — a strong signal of generator corruption. No matching Nigerian place exists. Removed.
9. **East African block.** Fifteen consecutive tokens (`Gurfuudle` … `Harardera`) are Somali/Kenyan place names identical to those appearing in other corrupted entries in this namebase file (cf. the `Tiro` entry, i=200194, and the Tawellemmet entry above it). They are mixer bleed and were removed wholesale.
10. **Synthetic block.** The final ten tokens are `Tera` + a CV syllable drawn from a fixed suffix inventory (`-wa, -ta, -bwe, -sa, -fa, -ga, -ja, -da, -zi, -bo`) — the identical inventory that produced `TawellemmetBerberla…mu` in i=200188 and `Tirowa…Tirobo` in i=200194. Removed wholesale.
11. **Attempt to reach 25.** Additional Yamaltu/Deba localities were checked against GeoNames: `Kulau`, `Laleko`, `Tsando`, `Gadawo`, `Dumbu`, `Baltongo`, `Boltongoyel`, `Kakkau`, `Lambam`, `Saruje`, `Jannawo`, `Kunnuwal`, `Kurjale`, `Birdeka`, `Lubo`, `Kinafa`, `Garin Kudi`, `Ruwa Biri`, `Dahirma` — **all returned zero Nigerian records**. `Baure`, `Pata`, `Kadi`, `Kuri`, `Kanawa`, `Beguwa`, `Poli`, `Zagaina`, `Shuwari`, `Maikaho` returned records only outside Gombe or only in the Jara Area / Bolewa country. Borno-side candidates (`Briyel`, `Jara-dali`, `Jara-gol`, `Teli`, `Wuyo`, `Balbaya`, `Gasi`) all fall in **Bayo/Shani/Hawul** LGAs, which are Jara/Bura/Hona territory rather than Tera. No further additions could be justified.

## Phonology Check

Tera (Tench 2007) has: implosives /ɓ ɓʲ ɗ ɠ/; prenasalised stops /ᵐb ⁿd ᶮdʒ ᵑɡ ᵑɡʷ/; lateral fricatives /ɬ ɮ/; labialised velars /kʷ ɡʷ xʷ ɣʷ/; /h/ only in English/Hausa loans; vowels /i ɨ e a o u/ (+ length); diphthongs /eu oi ai au/; three tones, unwritten.

- **Prenasalised / nasal-initial and nasal-medial forms**: Nono, Hinna, Shinga, Dangar, Lano — all legal.
- **/l/ and /r/ codas and medials**: Liji, Kalo, Kalshingi, Lano, Kurba, Wajari, Dangar — legal; /r/ is a plain alveolar trill in Tera.
- **Velar + /w/**: Gwani, Kwadon — matches Tera's labialised-velar series /kʷ ɡʷ/.
- **/h/ in Hinna** is consistent with Tench's note that /h/ entered Tera through Hausa/English contact, and Hinna is a long-standing Hausa-mediated spelling of the Pidlimdi centre.
- **Vowel inventory**: every retained name uses only a/e/i/o/u, no illegal vowel; no name has an initial consonant cluster.
- **Syllable shape**: all retained names are CV(C)CV(C)(CV) — e.g. *Ka-lo*, *De-ba*, *Zam-buk*, *Kal-shin-gi*, *Kwa-don*, *Wa-ja-ri* — matching Tera's CV/CVC template.
- **Removed items failing phonology/orthography**: `Ghәna` (Cyrillic schwa), `Terawa`-series (mechanical suffixes producing a repeated `Tera-` stem that no real toponym set would show), `Gurfuudle`/`Guriceel`/`Harardera` (Somali `-eel`, `-dh-`, geminate `uu` — impossible in Tera orthography).

## Mixer Map Check

- **Synthetic-token block**: 10 tokens `Tera{wa,ta,bwe,sa,fa,ga,ja,da,zi,bo}` — identical suffix inventory to the corrupted `Tawellemmet Berber` (i=200188) and `Tiro` (i=200194) entries. Confirmed generator artefact, removed.
- **East-African bleed block**: 15 tokens (`Gurfuudle, Dabare, Runni, Kachoda, Mkokoni, Kehancha, Sibiloi, Waamo, Giohar, Guriceel, Selina, Sindo, Galole, Kalokol, Harardera`) — the same Somali/Kenyan pool that contaminates `Tiro` and `Tawellemmet`. Removed.
- **Neighbouring-language bleed within Gombe**: Tangale (`Kaltungo`, `Billiri`), Pero (`Pero`, `Filiya`, `Gwandum`, `Gundale`), Waja (`Balanga`), Bole/Kwaami (`Kwami`, `Potiskum`), Hausa–Fulani emirate towns (`Dukku`, `Pindiga`, `Birnin Fulani`, `Jauro Gotel`). Removed.
- **Language/dialect names used as settlements**: `Tera`, `Pidlimdi`, `Bura Kokura`, `Nyemathi`, `Nimalto`, `Pero`. Removed.
- **Cross-border false friends**: `Difa` (looked like Diffa, Niger — turned out genuine and local, **retained**); `Boga` (Adamawa/Boga language — removed); `Tera` itself (Téra, Niger — removed).
- Final list contains no duplicates, no admin units, no physical features, no ethnonyms, no synthetic tokens.

## Final Verification

- 17/17 retained names are attested populated places in **Yamaltu/Deba LGA (Gombe State, "Tera and Fulani")** or, for `Doho`, in adjacent **Kwami LGA**, and 9 of the 17 (`Deba, Hinna, Gwani, Liji, Kalshingi, Zambuk, Kurba, Doho` + the dialect-name overlap) are named directly as Tera settlements by English Wikipedia's *Tera people* article.
- Count **17 < 25** → per Rule 3, status is **WAITING** with the verified subset retained in `b`.
- `min` = 4 (`Deba`, `Kalo`, `Difa`, `Wade`, `Lano`, `Nono`, `Liji`, `Doho`), `max` = 9 (`Kalshingi`), `d` = "", `m` = 0.
- Confidence **MEDIUM**: existence and location of all 17 are solid (GeoNames + Wikipedia), but for the 8 that are attested only as Yamaltu/Deba populated places (not named individually as Tera in a linguistic source) the Tera-vs-Fulani split inside the LGA cannot be resolved from open sources. To reach COMPLETE this entry needs a village-level Tera source — e.g. Blench's *Atlas of Nigerian Languages* (4th ed.) Tera entry, the Tera NT (2016) translation project's village list, or an SIL Tera survey report.
