**Minor note:** "Huizhao" — possibly a romanization of Huizhou (惠州), a major city in Guangdong. The spelling "Huizhao" follows older/alternative romanization but refers to the correct place.

### i=61 — Koya-Konda-Manda-Pengo

**Language:** Koya, Konda, Manda, Pengo — South-Central Dravidian languages (Gondi-Kui group)
**Native region:** Andhra Pradesh and Odisha, India
**Place names in b:** Visakhapatnam, Vizianagaram, Srikakulam, Koraput, Rayagada, Paralakhemundi, Gunupur, Malkangiri, Nabarangpur, Kalahandi, Bhawanipatna, Daspalla, Bissam Cuttack, Lakshmipur, Baliguda, Motu, Pottangi, Nandapur, Ramabhadrapuram, Araku, Bobbili, Salur, Padmapur, Jeypore, Paderu

**Verification:** All are real places in Andhra Pradesh and Odisha, India:
- Visakhapatnam — major port city in Andhra Pradesh (~2 million population)
- Vizianagaram, Srikakulam — cities in northern Andhra Pradesh (Uttarandhra region)
- Koraput, Malkangiri, Nabarangpur, Rayagada — towns in tribal districts of Odisha/Andhra
- Jeypore — town in Koraput district, Odisha
- Araku — valley/town in Andhra Pradesh (Araku Valley)
- Bobbili, Salur — towns in Vizianagaram district

All names follow Dravidian phonological patterns: -puram/-pur suffixes common in Telugu/Dravidian place names, retroflex consonants (in original forms), CV-CVCV structures.

**Concern:**
- Several are **district names** rather than cities/towns: Koraput, Kalahandi, Malkangiri, Nabarangpur, Rayagada, Srikakulam, Visakhapatnam, Vizianagaram are all districts. However, they are also the names of the headquarters towns, so they function as both. For fantasy map generation, using district names is acceptable.
- "Motu" and "Pottangi" are small towns/tribal villages — appropriate.

**STATUS:** OK
**ISSUES:** none (district/town overlap is noted but acceptable)

**Verification:** All are villages/settlements in Dagestan, Russia:
- Archi — the language name itself; also associated with Archib village
- Kubachi — famous village known for jewelry metalwork
- Khunzakh — village and plateau in Dagestan, historical significance
- Botlikh, Godoberi, Chamalal, Tindi, Bagulal — villages, each namesakes of small language/dialect groups
- Bezhta, Hunzib, Tsez (also spelled Tese/Dido) — villages and language namesakes
- Andi — village, namesake of Andic languages
- Gamiyakh, Karata — villages
- Akusha — village and district center
- Mugi — village
- Khvartseri — village
- Archib — the actual village where Archi language is spoken
- Khuchada, Talda, Gigatl, Chitab, Shalib — villages in Dagestan

Names follow Northeast Caucasian phonological patterns: ejective consonants, lateral fricatives, consonant-heavy clusters, uvular/epiglottal sounds — well reflected in transliteration.
